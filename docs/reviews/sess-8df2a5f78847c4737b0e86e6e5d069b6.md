## Mechanical failures

### 1. `ResponseHeaderTimeout: 30s` on the Gemini client kills a quarter of all vision calls — confirmed

**Confirmed against the code.** `internal/gemini/client.go:112` sets
`ResponseHeaderTimeout: 30 * time.Second` on the transport, copied from the
DeepSeek and Kimi clients. Those stream, so headers arrive in a second or two.
The Gemini call does not stream and spends most of its time thinking before it
answers: the *successful* calls in this session took 23.9s, 24.5s, 25.5s, 26.7s,
27.5s and 28.3s of tool time. They were passing under the wire by a second or
two.

Seven calls did not:

| sub-turn | tool | tool time | error |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 184 | ReviewScreenshot | 30.2s | `net/http: timeout awaiting response headers` |
| 199 | ReviewScreenshot | 30.2s | same |
| 200 | ReviewScreenshot | 30.2s | same |
| 211 | ReviewScreenshot | 30.2s | same |
| 213 | ReviewScreenshot | 30.4s | same |
| 214 | ReviewScreenshot | 30.2s | same |
| 215 | AskVision | 2.3s | `unexpected status 504: Deadline expired` (server-side) |

The 30.2s figure repeating to the decimal is the signature. `metrics.md` flagged
sub-turns 199–200 and 213–214 as "repeated-call — byte-identical arguments" and
198 four times over; every one of those repeats is the model reissuing a call the
transport had just cut off, not a loop of its own making.

The operator-facing setting is defeated by this.
`settings.KeyToolReviewScreenshotTimeout` defaults to `60s`
(`internal/settings/registry.go:179`) and `ReviewScreenshotTimeout` to the same
(`internal/tools/registry.go:46`), so an operator raising it changes nothing —
the transport gives up at 30s regardless.

**Cost to the run.** 7 wasted sub-turns and roughly **210s of wall clock, 8% of
the run**. The dollar cost is small (~$0.006) because a timed-out call bills
nothing, but each retry then paid full price for work Gemini may already have
done. Sub-turn 214 is the worst case: three consecutive timeouts (213, 214, 215)
on the dark-mode check before the model gave up on four images and split the
question into two smaller AskVision calls at 216–217.

**Fix.** Either drop `ResponseHeaderTimeout` from the Gemini client and let the
tool-level context bound the call — which is what the comment at
`client.go:96–98` says the design intends — or raise it above the configured
tool timeout. The comment ("transport-level timeouts bound connection setup,
matching internal/deepseek") describes the intent correctly; the 30s value does
not implement it for a non-streaming reasoning call.

### 3. `ReviewScreenshot` conversation continuation went 0 for 3 — hypothesis

The tool advertises follow-ups as the cheap path ("passing that id continues it
… so 'look closer at the header' costs one call rather than a fresh review",
`internal/tools/definitions.go:159`). The model tried three times and never
completed one:

- **183** — passed `conversation_id` + `spec`, refused: *"a follow-up call takes
  no spec: the spec is fixed when the conversation starts"*
  (`reviewscreenshot.go:191`). The refusal is correct in itself, but the model
  had been passing `spec` on every call for 60 sub-turns; nothing in the
  description says the field becomes illegal on continuation.
- **184** — retried without `spec`, hit finding 1's timeout.
- After that it never attempted a continuation again. Every subsequent review
  (185, 190, 212, 223) opened a fresh conversation and re-uploaded four images.

I cannot say what the continuation would have cost, because it never returned
once. The evidence is that the feature has now survived two production reviews
without a single successful use.

## What the vision path actually bought

Three findings that no deterministic check in this repo could have produced.
Ranked by what they were worth:

### 1. The eval-run comparison table was silently broken (sub-turn 223, $0.0313)

The best call of the session. Reviewing the "after" screenshots, Gemini reported
the eval-run comparison rendering as *"a static horizontal bar of headers with
the metrics stacked vertically below them"*. The model disbelieved it — its
reasoning at 224 works through three reasons the CSS should have been scoped
correctly — then used `AskVision` to describe the same image, then wrote a DOM
probe at 225 and confirmed it: `trDisplay: flex, tdDisplay: block`.

The cause was real and would have shipped: the pre-existing ≤560px block's
`.session-table tr` / `td` card rules are **unscoped**, so they hit the eval
tables too. The model's own `.eval-run thead` restore had made it *worse* —
visible headers over carded rows. No probe would have found this: nothing
overflowed, nothing was undersized, nothing was past the viewport. Only looking
at it worked.

### 2. `[data-slot="toggle-group-item"]` never got the 44px rule (sub-turn 190, part of $0.0331)

Gemini reported the start-form permission toggles at "approximately 32 to 36px".
The model was by this point actively distrusting Gemini's pixel claims — and
went and measured anyway, discovering its `[data-slot="toggle"]` rule missed
shadcn's actual attribute, `toggle-group-item`. The toggles really were 36px.
The probe had missed them because it never clicked the form open.

Worth noting how narrowly this landed. Of the four findings in that same call,
three were wrong; this one was right. Had the model applied its "the probe is
ground truth" heuristic uniformly, it would have discarded the one true finding
with the three false ones.

### 3. The top nav was stacking into five rows (sub-turn 151, $0.0241)

An `AskVision` call on a nav-only capture returned a clean structural read:
wordmark, links, Start-run, search and LIVE badge each on their own row. That
single answer is what produced the two-row nav redesign — the largest visual
improvement in the diff. It is exactly the "observer, not defect detector" role
the previous review said the path was missing, and `AskVision` filled it.

## Where the vision model was wrong

**Every size estimate it volunteered was wrong except one.** Collected:

| sub-turn | Gemini claimed | actual (measured) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 126 | empty-chat "open" button ~24px | a badge, not interactive |
| 153 | Send button ~50x25 | before the fix; directionally right |
| 185 | Start run / Sessions tab / Start an eval "36px" | 44px |
| 190 | settings chips and Expand all "32 to 36px" | 44px |
| 190 | permission toggles "32 to 36px" | **36px — correct** |
| 201 | nav links and LIVE badge "below the 40px threshold" | 44px (verified at 202) |

Five false claims, one true one. The model handled this well — it built a
Playwright probe at 127 and thereafter treated the DOM as ground truth — but each
false claim still cost a sub-turn or two of reasoning and, at 186–189 and
202, a fresh DOM check to disprove.

**`AskVision`'s description invites exactly this.** It tells the model to *"Ask
for what you can act on — an element, a position, **a measurement**, a yes or
no"* (`internal/tools/definitions.go:179`). On this evidence, a measurement is
the one thing it should never be asked for.

Two smaller misreads, both correctly discarded by the model:

- **121 ($0.0456, the most expensive call of the session)** — three
  high-confidence findings, all false. It read a transcript stream scrolled to
  its tail as "the sticky header covers the top of the scrollable content", twice,
  and called the floating "Jump to live" pill an overlap defect. The model's
  reasoning at 122 diagnoses this precisely: *"the vision model misread normal
  scrolled state as 'sticky header covers content'."* Correct, and it moved on
  without changing anything. But $0.0456 — 8% of the session — bought zero
  changes.
- **153** — asked about the work-requests table, it described the stuck-sessions
  table instead, because the model's `selector: ".ops-table"` captured the first
  of three matching elements. The tool did what it was told; the question and
  the image had drifted apart, and neither side flagged it.

## Ranked fixes

1. **Remove or raise `ResponseHeaderTimeout` on the Gemini client.**
   `internal/gemini/client.go:112`. *Harness bug.* Would have removed 7 failed
   calls, 7 sub-turns and ~210s (8%) of wall clock from this run, and would make
   `tools.reviewscreenshot_timeout` mean something. Highest-value fix on this
   list by a wide margin, and a one-line change.
2. **Stop asking the vision model for measurements; say so in both
   descriptions.** `internal/tools/definitions.go:159, 179`. *Tool description.*
   Strike "a measurement" from `AskVision`'s list of things worth asking for, and
   add to both: sizes, distances and pixel values are estimated from a downscaled
   image and are frequently wrong by 20% or more — measure them from the DOM and
   ask the reviewer about structure, occlusion, alignment and contrast instead.
   Five false findings in this session, each costing a sub-turn or two to
   disprove.
3. **Make the follow-up path usable, or drop it.** `reviewscreenshot.go:188–191`.
   *Harness.* Accept and ignore a `spec` that matches the conversation's own (or
   name the legal fields in the refusal). Zero successful continuations across
   two reviewed sessions is a feature that is costing description tokens on every
   request head and buying nothing.
4. **Reconsider `mode: describe` now that `AskVision` exists.** *Tool surface.*
   One use, $0.0249, an element inventory with no layout content, never used
   again. `AskVision` does the same job better and cheaper. Retiring it would
   shorten the request head.
5. **Warn that `selector` takes the first match.**
   `internal/tools/definitions.go:192`. *Tool description.* One wasted vision
   call at 153 where the question and the image were about different tables and
   nothing said so. Naming the match count in the result line
   (`clipped to .ops-table — 3 elements matched, captured the first`) would close
   it at the source.
6. **Give the model the pattern it worked out for itself.** *Prompt / docs.* By
   sub-turn 127 it had settled on the right division of labour — a Playwright DOM
   probe for sizes, overflow and computed style; vision for structure, occlusion
   and "does this look broken". That division is what made the session work, and
   it cost ~15 sub-turns to discover. `docs/gemini-3.5-flash-ui-review-prompting.md`
   is the place to write it down.
